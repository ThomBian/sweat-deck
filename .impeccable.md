# Impeccable — Sweat Deck

> **Status:** Confirmed. All future design passes should follow **Design Context** and **References** below.

## Design Context

### Users

- **Who:** **General population**—anyone who wants training to feel **fun**, not like building a complicated program. No expectation of being an athlete or power user.
- **Context:** Quick sessions, phone in hand; may be new to structured workouts or avoiding “trainingsplan” complexity.
- **Job to be done:** Start fast, stay engaged, feel **rewarded** for showing up and finishing cards—without learning a dense UI or a bunch of rules up front.

### Brand personality (3 words)

**fun · rewarding · encouraging**

- Game rules can still be tough (see `docs/CONCEPT.md`); the **voice and visuals** cheer progress, celebrate draws and finishes, and avoid shaming or cold “merciless” tone in copy and affordances.
- Playful micro-moments (card reveal, streaks, small wins) should feel **earned**, not noisy or childish.

### Emotional direction

**Playful** and **rewarding**—light energy, clear feedback when something good happens (card done, break started, session complete). Tension comes from the *game*, not from a stern interface.

### References (locked)

| **Like**    | **Figma**   | Calm canvas, clear focus, generous space, confident chrome, fast “get in and work” | Don’t paste Figma’s literal UI; borrow *feel*: clarity, breath, one job per view. |

### Aesthetic direction

- **Visual anchor:** Physical **playing cards** (suits, stack, flip)—a **deck game** that happens to be a workout, not a spreadsheet with icons.
- **Color:** **Colorful**—no fixed brand palette required. Prefer **OKLCH**, multiple well-separated hues (suits, states, accents), neutrals **tinted** toward the dominant brand hue once chosen. Avoid the cliché “one neon accent on black” as the *only* expression of color. No locked must-use / must-avoid colors unless you add them later.
- **Theme:** Dark *or* light can work; **playful + colorful** often reads well with a **dark base** and **saturated accents** (keeps contrast for sweat/glare). If we add light mode, keep the same playful reward moments—not a washed-out admin theme.
- **Anti-patterns (product + CSS):** No generic “AI fitness” cyan/purple glow stack, no gradient text, no thick **left/right color stripe** callouts (see impeccable **absolute_bans**). No Salesforce-like data walls during play.

### Design principles

1. **One primary action** on the workout path: draw → read → move. Secondary actions stay quiet.
2. **Glanceable under fatigue:** big type where it counts, short labels, tap targets for real hands; respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
3. **Reward in the open:** finish states, breaks, and milestones get **visible delight** (motion, color, copy)—the app should feel happy *with* the user.
4. **Simple entry:** defaults and flows that don’t require configuring a “training builder” before the first card.
5. **Deck metaphor over dashboard:** suits, stack height, and history stay **material**—not decorative fitness stock art.
6. **Accessibility:** Aim for **WCAG AA** for text contrast as we add color; don’t rely on color alone for state.

### Technical constraints (known)

- **Stack:** Vite, React, TypeScript, Tailwind v4, shadcn/ui (Base UI), Framer Motion, PWA.
- **Motion:** Prefer `transform` / `opacity`; meaningful moments (flip, summary) over random micro-jitter.
